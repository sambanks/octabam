Stock Octatrack never shows you the wave. Level meters, the ear, maybe a
scope on the outs — that is it. Inside the box you cannot see which track
is actually putting energy out, whether L and R are fighting, or whether
something is clipping *before* it hits MAIN. You guess.

SCOPE puts the waveform on the unit's own screen while you play. No
computer, no extra box, no leaving the instrument. That is the whole
point: the shape of the sound, on the glass, under your fingers.

**DOWN + TEMPO** opens; **UP + TEMPO** is still TUNER. **UP** / **DOWN**
cycle **OSC** (mono polyline), **XY** (L×R Lissajous), and **ALL** (4×2
mini OSC — T1–T4 left, T5–T8 right, centre divider; T1 top-left). **A**
GAIN, **B** TIME. **CUE** release toggles the G/P HUD on OSC / XY (ALL
has none). Peak / `!` flags clipping early.

**ALL** is the killer view for a busy project: eight lanes at once, so a
silent track is obvious and a hot one lights up before it cooks the bus.
**XY** shows correlation / phase between L and R. **OSC** is the classic
single-trace look when you want one signal big.

**Hardware (author MKII, `LSCOPE BJ`, 24 Oct 2026):** busy 8-track play +
CF METER — SCOPE open ISR mean 291 µs / closed 288 µs, TUE 0 / ROE 0;
several minutes ALL open with no glitch.
